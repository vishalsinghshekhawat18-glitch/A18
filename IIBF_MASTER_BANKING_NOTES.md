## 1.1 Nomination under Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025
*Statutory Reference: Amendments to Banking Regulation Act 1949, RBI Act 1934, and State Bank of India Act 1955 | Effective Date: November 1, 2025 (Notified: October 23, 2025)*

### A. Context & Purpose
Under the historic framework (Section 45ZA to 45ZF of the BR Act), a depositor could only nominate **one single individual** per bank account or locker. In the event of the depositor's demise, multiple legal heirs frequently disputed the nominee's receipt of funds, causing prolonged litigation and thousands of crores accumulating in the Depositor Education and Awareness (DEA) Fund. The **Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025** overhauled this architecture.

### B. Core Provisions
1. **Multiple Nominations (Up to 4 Nominees):**
   - Customers may now appoint **up to four nominees** for a single deposit account, safe custody article, or safe deposit locker.
2. **Two Nomination Modalities:**
   - **Simultaneous Nomination:** The depositor appoints up to four nominees at the same time and explicitly assigns the percentage share of entitlement for each (e.g., Nominee A: 40%, Nominee B: 30%, Nominee C: 30%). The sum total must equal exactly 100%.
   - **Successive (Sequential) Nomination:** The depositor ranks up to four nominees in consecutive order of priority (Nominee 1 -> Nominee 2 -> Nominee 3 -> Nominee 4). Nominee 2 only receives entitlement if Nominee 1 predeceases the depositor.
3. **Safe Deposit Lockers & Safe Custody Articles:**
   - For **single hirers**, successive nomination is permitted (Nominee 1, failing whom Nominee 2).
   - For **joint hirers** (operated under 'Either or Survivor' or 'Jointly'), nomination operates to deliver the contents to the survivor(s) along with the nominee(s) of the deceased hirer, protecting banks against wrongful conversion claims.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                    NOMINATION REFORMS AT A GLANCE (2025)                   │
├────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Maximum Nominees Allowed   │ 4 Nominees per Account / Locker               │
│ Modalities Supported       │ Simultaneous (with % shares) OR Successive    │
│ Share Allocation Rule      │ Total percentage shares must equal 100.0%     │
│ Minor Nominee Rule         │ Appointee/Guardian mandatory during minority  │
│ Effective Date             │ 1st November 2025                             │
└────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

> 🎯 **Exam Focus & Legal Trap:**
> A nominee acts as a **trustee** of the legal heirs, NOT the absolute beneficial owner (Supreme Court ruling in *Smt. Sarbati Devi vs Smt. Usha Devi*). The bank obtains a valid statutory discharge upon paying the nominee under Section 45ZA, but the legal heirs retain the right to claim the estate from the nominee under succession law.

## 1.2 RBI Revised Norms for Deposit Accounts of Minors
*Regulatory Reference: RBI Master Direction on Interest Rate on Deposits / Opening of Deposit Accounts | Applicable to: All Commercial Banks, RRBs, SFBs, and Co-operative Banks*

### A. Core Provisions
1. **Natural Guardianship (Mothers Recognized):**
   - Minors of any age are permitted to open and operate Savings and Term Deposit accounts through their natural or legal guardian.
   - The RBI explicitly mandates that banks **must accept the mother as a natural guardian** for opening and operating minor accounts on par with the father (aligning with the landmark SC verdict in *Githa Hariharan vs RBI*).
2. **Self-Operated Accounts by Minors (Age 10+ Years):**
   - Minors who have attained the age of **10 years** may be allowed to open and operate savings bank accounts independently in their own name, provided they are literate.
   - Banks are granted discretion to fix minimum balance requirements, maximum deposit limits, and per-day transaction ceilings based on internal risk management policies.
3. **Cheque Book & Digital Access:**
   - Banks may offer ATM/Debit cards, mobile banking, and internet banking to minors aged 10+ with strict daily debit velocity limits.
4. **Prohibition of Overdraft / Credit Facilities:**
   - Under Section 11 of the **Indian Contract Act, 1872**, a minor is incompetent to contract. Consequently, **no overdraft, loan, or credit facility can ever be granted to a minor account**, and accounts cannot be allowed to run into debit balances under any circumstances.
5. **Procedure upon Attaining Majority (Age 18):**
   - The operation of the account by the guardian automatically ceases.
   - The newly major individual must submit fresh KYC documents, fresh specimen signatures, and written confirmation of the account balance before resuming operations.

## 1.3 DICGC Deposit Insurance & Risk-Based Premium Architecture
*Statutory Reference: Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation Act, 1961 (DICGC Act) & Section 15(1) Master Amendments*

### A. Scope & Coverage Limits
- **Maximum Insurance Limit:** **₹5,00,000 (Rupees Five Lakh)** per depositor per insured bank (inclusive of both Principal and Accrued Interest across all domestic branches).
- **Insured Institutions:** All Commercial Banks (Public, Private, Foreign bank branches in India), Local Area Banks (LABs), Small Finance Banks (SFBs), Payment Banks (PBs), Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), and all eligible Urban/State/District Central Co-operative Banks.
- **Excluded Deposits:**
  1. Deposits of foreign governments.
  2. Deposits of Central and State Governments.
  3. Inter-bank deposits (deposits placed by one bank with another bank).
  4. Deposits held outside India.
  5. Any amount due on account of any transfer from India to outside India.

### B. The 90-Day Mandatory Payout Mandate (Section 18A)
Introduced under the *DICGC (Amendment) Act, 2021*, Section 18A guarantees depositors swift liquidity when an insured bank is placed under All-Inclusive Directions (AID) or moratorium by the RBI:
- **First 45 Days:** The troubled bank must compile, verify, and submit the complete list of eligible depositor claims to DICGC.
- **Next 45 Days:** DICGC verifies claims and directly transfers up to ₹5,00,000 into the alternate bank accounts of depositors.
- **Total Guaranteed Timeline:** Maximum **90 Days** from the date of RBI directions.

### C. Transition to Risk-Based Premium (RBP)
- **Extant Baseline Premium:** Flat rate of **12 paise per ₹100 of assessable deposits per annum** (0.12% p.a.), payable semi-annually.
- **New RBP Framework (Effective FY27):**
  - Replaces flat pricing with a differential rating linked to CAMELS (Capital, Asset Quality, Management, Earnings, Liquidity, Systems & Control) supervision scores.
  - **Tier 1 / Tier 2 Incentive Models:** Well-capitalized banks with low Net NPAs receive up to **33.33% risk discount** and up to **25% vintage stability discount**, encouraging sound risk management.

## 1.4 SEBI Revised Nomination Norms for Demat & Mutual Funds
*Regulatory Reference: SEBI Circular on Investor Operational Protections | Effective 2025*

1. **Incapacitated Single Holder Relief:**
   - Single-holding mutual fund folio investors and demat account holders can authorize **one nominee (excluding minors)** to operate their accounts if the investor becomes **physically incapacitated** but remains mentally capable of contracting.
2. **Strict Operational Safeguards:**
   - An authorized official of the Asset Management Company (AMC) or Depository Participant (DP) must physically visit the incapacitated person at their residence/hospital to record consent, collect medical proof, and verify identity.
   - **Zero Alteration Rule:** The nominee is strictly barred from modifying the registered mobile number, email address, or linked bank account.
   - **Redemption Destination:** All redemption proceeds must flow **exclusively into the pre-existing verified bank account** linked to the investor's folio.

## 1.5 Reserve Bank - Integrated Ombudsman Scheme (RB-IOS) 2026
*Regulatory Reference: RBI (Internal Ombudsman & Integrated Ombudsman) Master Directions 2026 | Effective Date: July 1, 2026*

### A. Integrated "One Nation One Ombudsman" Architecture
RB-IOS 2026 unifies customer grievance redressal across all RBI-regulated entities into a single, seamless, cost-free dispute resolution mechanism.
- **Entities Covered:**
  - All Scheduled Commercial Banks (SCBs), Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), Local Area Banks (LABs), Small Finance Banks (SFBs), and Payment Banks (PBs).
  - All Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) with public customer interface and asset size $\ge$ ₹100 Crore.
  - Primary (Urban) Co-operative Banks with deposits $\ge$ ₹50 Crore.
  - Credit Information Companies (CICs) and all Payment System Participants (PSPs).

### B. Compensation & Key Changes over 2021 Scheme
1. **Compensation Ceiling Raised:**
   - Direct consequential loss compensation increased from **₹20 Lakh to ₹30 Lakh**.
   - Additional compensation up to **₹1 Lakh** for mental harassment, loss of time, and out-of-pocket expenses.
2. **Tenure & Governance:**
   - RBI Ombudsman / Deputy Ombudsman appointed for a fixed tenure of **3 to 5 years** (upper age limit: 70 years).
3. **Centralised Receipt and Processing Centre (CRPC):**
   - Operationalized at RBI Chandigarh for single-point physical and digital ingestion of all complaints nationwide.
4. **Appellate Authority:** Executive Director in-charge of the Consumer Education and Protection Department (CEPD) at RBI Central Office.

## 2.1 Expected Credit Loss (ECL) Framework (Ind-AS 109 / IFRS 9)
*Regulatory Reference: RBI Discussion Paper & Final Prudential Framework on Dynamic Provisioning and ECL*

### A. Paradigm Shift: Incurred Loss vs Expected Loss
- **Traditional Incurred Loss Model (IRAC Norms):** Provisions were made only **after** an asset suffered a default or became an NPA (e.g., 90 days past due). This led to pro-cyclicality — banks recognized losses too late during economic downturns ("too little, too late").
- **Forward-Looking ECL Model (Ind-AS 109):** Mandates banks to assess credit risk and provide for expected future default losses from the **very moment a loan is originated**, incorporating macroeconomic forecasts.

### B. The Three-Stage Impairment Model

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                    ECL THREE-STAGE CLASSIFICATION MATRIX                   │
├─────────┬──────────────────────┬──────────────────────┬────────────────────┤
│ Stage   │ Asset Credit Status  │ Trigger Condition    │ Provisioning Basis │
├─────────┼──────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼────────────────────┤
│ Stage 1 │ Performing           │ 0 to 30 Days Past    │ 12-Month ECL       │
│         │ (Standard Normal)    │ Due (No SICR)        │                    │
├─────────┼──────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼────────────────────┤
│ Stage 2 │ Underperforming      │ 31 to 89 Days Past   │ Lifetime ECL       │
│         │ (SICR Identified)    │ Due OR Rating Drop   │                    │
├─────────┼──────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼────────────────────┤
│ Stage 3 │ Credit-Impaired      │ 90+ Days Past Due    │ Lifetime ECL       │
│         │ (Default / NPA)      │ (Objective Default)  │ (High LGD)         │
└─────────┴──────────────────────┴──────────────────────┴────────────────────┘
```

### C. Quantitative ECL Formula
$$\text{ECL} = \text{PD (Probability of Default)} \times \text{LGD (Loss Given Default)} \times \text{EAD (Exposure at Default)}$$
- **PD:** Likelihood of borrower defaulting over 12 months or remaining life.
- **LGD:** Percentage of economic exposure lost if default occurs (considering collateral haircut and recovery costs).
- **EAD:** Total gross carrying amount outstanding at default (including undrawn committed credit lines).

## 2.2 RBI Co-Lending Model (CLM 1 & CLM 2)
*Regulatory Reference: RBI/2020-21/63 FIDD.CO.Plan.BC.No.8/04.09.01/2020-21*

### A. Strategic Objective
To combine the **low-cost funds of Commercial Banks** with the **last-mile sourcing and recovery reach of NBFCs** to deliver affordable credit to Priority Sector borrowers.

### B. Operational Models
1. **CLM Option 1 (Joint Lending in Real Time):**
   - The Bank and NBFC jointly evaluate and sanction the loan under a pre-agreed master lending framework.
2. **CLM Option 2 (Direct Assignment / Takeover):**
   - The NBFC originates the loan on its books based on agreed underwriting standards, and the Bank takes over its share on a back-to-back basis within a pre-specified window.

### C. Invariant Regulatory Mandates
- **Minimum 20% Risk Retention:** The NBFC **must retain a minimum 20% share** of individual loans on its own balance sheet. The Bank cannot take more than 80%.
- **Blended Interest Rate:** The end borrower pays a single weighted-average blended rate:
  $$\text{Blended Rate} = (\text{Bank Share } \% \times \text{Bank Rate}) + (\text{NBFC Share } \% \times \text{NBFC Rate})$$
- **Escrow Account Routing:** All customer repayments must flow into a designated tripartite Escrow Account to ensure transparent proportional split of principal and interest.
- **No Third-Party Guarantees:** Banks cannot take credit guarantees or First Loss Default Guarantees (FLDG) from the co-lending NBFC beyond RBI regulatory caps.

## 2.3 Fair Practices Code on Penal Charges in Loan Accounts
*Regulatory Reference: RBI/2023-24/53 DoR.MCS.REC.28/01.01.001/2023-24 | Effective Date: April 1, 2024*

1. **Abolition of Penal Interest:**
   - Banks and NBFCs are strictly prohibited from levying **penal interest** (i.e., increasing the headline interest rate charged on the principal).
   - Penalties for non-compliance with loan covenants (e.g., late EMI, non-submission of stock statements) can only be recovered as **'Penal Charges'**.
2. **No Capitalization / No Compounding:**
   - **Penal charges shall NOT be capitalized.** They cannot be added to the principal balance outstanding, and no interest can ever be computed on penal charges.
3. **Fairness & Transparency:**
   - Board-approved policy specifying objective penal charges.
   - For loans to individual borrowers (for non-business purposes), penal charges cannot exceed the charges levied on non-individual borrowers for identical defaults.

## 2.5 Mutual Credit Guarantee Scheme (MCGS) & CGTMSE Architecture
*Nodal Agency: Ministry of MSME, SIDBI, and National Credit Guarantee Trustee Company (NCGTC)*

1. **MCGS for Equipment & Working Capital:**
   - Credit guarantee coverage for credit facilities extended to Micro and Small Enterprises up to **₹5 Crore** (enhanced to **₹10 Crore** for select high-tech/export manufacturing).
2. **Tiered Guarantee Coverage Rates:**
   - **Up to ₹5 Lakh (Micro Enterprises):** 85% coverage.
   - **Women Entrepreneurs, SC/ST, Aspirational Districts, NER:** Up to 85% coverage.
   - **Standard MSE Loans (Above ₹50 Lakh up to ₹5 Cr):** 75% coverage.
3. **Annual Guarantee Fee (AGF):** Standardized risk-based fee structure starting from 0.37% p.a. linked to NPA history of lending institution.

## 2.6 Interest Support Schemes (MISS & PM Vishwakarma)

### A. Modified Interest Subvention Scheme (MISS) for Agriculture
- **Eligible Facility:** Short-Term Crop Loans and working capital for Animal Husbandry & Fisheries up to **₹3,00,000 (₹3 Lakh)** per farmer via Kisan Credit Card (KCC).
- **Headline Benchmark Rate:** Fixed lending rate of **7.0% p.a.** by rural and commercial financial institutions.
- **Interest Subvention to Banks:** **1.5% p.a.** reimbursed to lending institutions by GoI.
- **Prompt Repayment Incentive (PRI):** **3.0% p.a.** credited directly to farmers who repay on or before due date.
- **Effective Net Cost to Farmer:** **4.0% p.a. ($7\% - 3\% = 4\%$)**.

### B. PM Vishwakarma Credit Scheme
- **Target Beneficiaries:** Traditional artisans and craftspeople across 18 designated trades.
- **Collateral-Free Enterprise Credit:**
  - *Tranche 1:* Up to ₹1,00,000 (18-month repayment).
  - *Tranche 2:* Up to ₹2,00,000 (30-month repayment, upon successful Tranche 1 servicing).
- **Concessional Interest:** Subsidized to **5.0% p.a.** for beneficiary; Ministry of MSME pays interest subvention up to **8.0% p.a.** directly to banks.

## 2.7 Priority Sector Lending (PSL) Comprehensive Overhaul (2025-26)
*Regulatory Reference: RBI Master Directions on Priority Sector Lending - Targets and Classification*

### A. Housing Loan PSL Limits (Population-Tiered)
To direct credit to affordable urban and rural housing, RBI revised the loan limits eligible for priority sector status:
1. **Centres with Population $\ge$ 50 Lakh (Tier 1 Metros):** Loans up to **₹50 Lakh** (overall dwelling unit cost capped at ₹65 Lakh).
2. **Centres with Population 10 Lakh to 50 Lakh:** Loans up to **₹45 Lakh** (overall cost capped at ₹55 Lakh).
3. **Centres with Population < 10 Lakh (Rural / Semi-Urban):** Loans up to **₹35 Lakh** (overall cost capped at ₹45 Lakh).

### B. UCBs PSL Target
- Urban Co-operative Banks must achieve **60% of ANBC (Adjusted Net Bank Credit)** or CEOBE for Priority Sector.
- Removed arbitrary loan ceilings on lending to individual women beneficiaries to foster financial independence.

### C. Renewable Energy PSL Limits
- Commercial solar/wind/biomass power plants and public utilities: Up to **₹35 Crore** per borrower.
- Individual households (rooftop solar installations): Up to **₹10 Lakh** per borrower.

## 3.1 RBI Revised Liquidity Management Framework (LMF)
*Regulatory Reference: RBI Revised Operating Procedures for Liquidity Management*

### A. Operating Target & Anchor
- **Operating Target:** The **Weighted Average Call Rate (WACR)** continues to be the explicit operating target of RBI monetary policy, anchored around the Repo Rate.
- **Discontinuation of 14-Day VRR/VRRR as Main Anchor:** The 14-day Variable Rate Repo/Reverse Repo auction is no longer the primary tool for short-term transient liquidity.
- **7-Day VRR/VRRR as New Anchor:** Short-term liquidity is now primarily managed through **7-day Variable Rate Repo (VRR)** and **7-day Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR)** auctions, complemented by overnight to 14-day fine-tuning operations.

### B. The Monetary Policy Corridor Structure
The Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) corridor is symmetric around the Policy Repo Rate:
1. **Ceiling (MSF Rate):** Marginal Standing Facility = $\text{Repo Rate} + 25 \text{ bps}$. Uncollateralized overnight emergency borrowing against SLR dip.
2. **Centre (Policy Repo Rate):** Key benchmark policy signaling rate.
3. **Floor (SDF Rate):** Standing Deposit Facility = $\text{Repo Rate} - 25 \text{ bps}$. Uncollateralized overnight liquidity absorption (introduced 2022).
4. **Corridor Width:** Fixed at **50 basis points (0.50%)**.

```
   [MSF Rate: Repo + 25 bps]  ◄── CEILING (Emergency borrowing)
              ▲
              │   (Corridor Width = 50 bps)
              ▼
       [POLICY REPO RATE]     ◄── ANCHOR (Policy signaling)
              ▲
              │   (Corridor Width = 50 bps)
              ▼
   [SDF Rate: Repo - 25 bps]  ◄── FLOOR (Uncollateralized absorption)
```

## 3.2 Financial Conditions Index (FCI) & Dynamic Factor Modeling
*Research Reference: RBI Monetary Policy Report & Working Paper Series*

### A. Concept & Purpose
The **Financial Conditions Index (FCI)** is a comprehensive composite metric computed by RBI using a **Dynamic Factor Model (DFM)** to gauge overall financial tightness or accommodation across the economy.
- **Interpretation:**
  - **Rising / Positive FCI:** Indicates **tightening** of financial conditions (higher yields, wider spreads, reduced credit liquidity).
  - **Falling / Negative FCI:** Indicates **accommodative / easy** financial conditions (lower spreads, abundant liquidity, supportive asset prices).

### B. The 5 Constituent Market Pillars

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                    RBI FINANCIAL CONDITIONS INDEX (FCI)                    │
├────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Money Market    │ • Weighted Average Money Market Rate (WAMMR) spread   │
│                    │ • WAMMR Volatility                                    │
│                    │ • Net LAF / NDTL ratio                                │
```

## 4.1 Unified Payments Interface (UPI) Advanced Innovations
*Nodal Body: National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI)*

1. **UPI Lite (On-Device Wallet):**
   - Designed for low-value transactions without querying core banking system (CBS).
   - Maximum Wallet Balance: **₹2,00,00**.
   - Maximum Per-Transaction Limit: **₹500**.
   - Requires zero UPI PIN, resulting in zero CBS transaction fatigue and near-zero failure rates.
2. **UPI 123PAY for Feature Phones:**
   - Enables digital payments without internet connection via 4 channels:
     1. Interactive Voice Response (IVR) numbers.
     2. Missed call-based merchant payment.
     3. Proximity sound-based soundwave data transmission.
     4. Pre-installed OEM feature phone apps.
3. **UPI Circle (Delegated Payments):**
   - Primary account holder authorizes secondary users (family members/dependents) to make payments from their bank balance under two modes:
     - *Full Delegation:* Secondary user initiates and executes within a pre-set spending limit.
     - *Partial Delegation:* Secondary user initiates, but primary user enters PIN on their phone to approve.
4. **Credit Lines on UPI:**
   - Pre-approved credit lines from scheduled commercial banks linked directly to UPI handles, converting UPI into a digital credit card rail.

## 4.3 Finternet: Unified Ledger Architecture (BIS Model)
*Conceptualized by: Bank for International Settlements (Agustin Carstens) & Nandan Nilekani*

### A. Core Definition
**Finternet** is an interconnected network of multiple financial ecosystems operating on a **Unified Programmable Ledger**. It integrates tokenized commercial bank deposits, central bank digital currencies (CBDCs), tokenized government securities, and real-world assets (RWAs) into a single interoperable environment.

### B. The Three U's of Finternet:
1. **User-Centric:** Puts the end-user in full control of their financial data, assets, and identity without proprietary platform lock-in.
2. **Unified:** Seamlessly connects diverse asset classes and financial institutions on programmable smart contracts.
3. **Universal:** Interoperable across jurisdictions, currencies, and technical architectures.

## 4.7 Stablecoins, CBDC (e-Rupee) & Crypto Regulatory Landscape
1. **Stablecoin Mechanism:** Cryptocurrencies pegged to a reserve asset (e.g., USD, Gold) — Fiat-collateralized (Tether USDT, Circle USDC), Crypto-collateralized (MakerDAO Dai), or Synthetic/Algorithmic.
2. **Global Regulatory Approaches:**
   - *European Union:* Markets in Crypto-Assets (**MiCA**) Regulation enforces 100% liquid reserve backing and redemption rights.
   - *United States:* **GENIUS Act** mandates reserve audits and prohibits misleading claims of Federal Deposit Insurance.
   - *China:* Absolute ban on crypto transactions, payments, and settlements.
3. **India's Sovereign Stance:**
   - Private cryptocurrencies and stablecoins are **not legal tender** in India.
   - RBI highlights severe macroeconomic threats: Currency substitution, erosion of monetary policy sovereignty, depletion of foreign exchange reserves, and systemic runs on commercial bank deposits.
   - **Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC - Digital Rupee / e₹):** RBI's official sovereign digital token issued on Distributed Ledger Technology across Retail (e₹-R) and Wholesale (e₹-W) pilots.

## 5.1 BRSR Core (Business Responsibility & Sustainability Reporting)
*Regulatory Reference: SEBI Regulation 34(2)(f) LODR | Applicable to: Top 1,000 Listed Entities*

### A. Framework Structure
BRSR replaces the earlier Business Responsibility Report (BRR) and is structured around the **9 Principles of the National Guidelines on Responsible Business Conduct (NGRBC)**.

### B. The BRSR Core Assurance Mandate
BRSR Core is a critical subset of high-priority quantitative KPIs subject to **mandatory reasonable assurance** by an independent certified third-party assurance provider:
1. **Greenhouse Gas (GHG) Footprint:** Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions and intensity per crore of turnover.
2. **Water Usage:** Total water consumed, percentage recycled, and water intensity.
3. **Waste Management:** Hazardous and non-hazardous waste generation and recycling ratios.
4. **Employee Gender Diversity:** Women employee percentage and POSH complaint resolution.
5. **Living Wages & Fair Compensation:** Ratio of median wage to minimum wage, and wage parity across genders.
6. **Gross Wages to Women:** Percentage of total wage bill paid to female employees.
7. **Supply Chain / Value Chain Footprint:** ESG disclosures extended to top suppliers (top 250 listed companies).

## 5.3 Basel Committee Climate-Related Financial Risk (CRFR) Templates
*Regulatory Reference: BCBS Consultative Document on Pillar 3 Disclosure of Climate Risks*

The Basel Committee standardizes climate risk disclosures across **6 Mandatory Templates**:

## 5.4 IFSCA Principles on Prevention of Greenwashing
*Regulatory Reference: International Financial Services Centres Authority (GIFT City, Gandhinagar)*

To safeguard GIFT City's status as a global sustainable finance hub, IFSCA notified **4 Core Anti-Greenwashing Principles** for all green bonds and ESG funds:
1. **Authenticity of Objective:** Stated environmental goals must be real, measurable, and verified against international standards.
2. **High Transparency:** Complete disclosure of fund allocation, carbon offsets utilized, and environmental KPIs.
3. **Truth in Marketing:** Absolute prohibition of misleading claims, generic buzzwords, or unsubstantiated "eco-friendly" labels.
4. **Independent Monitoring:** Continuous tracking and annual reporting by certified environmental auditors.

## 5.5 Social Stock Exchange (SSE) & Zero Coupon Zero Principal Instruments
*Regulatory Reference: SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations & SEBI (LODR) Amendments*

### A. Institutional Framework
SSE operates as a dedicated, regulated trading segment on the **BSE** and **NSE** to enable social enterprises to raise capital transparently.
- **Eligible Entities:**
  1. **Not-for-Profit Organisations (NPOs):** Registered trusts, societies, Section 8 companies.
  2. **For-Profit Social Enterprises (FPSEs):** Companies, LLPs working in 17 designated social development activities (eradicating hunger, healthcare, education, livelihood, gender equality).
- **Eligibility Criterion:** At least **67% of the entity's revenue, expenditure, or customer base** must be dedicated to eligible social activities over the preceding 3 financial years.

### B. Zero Coupon Zero Principal (ZCZP) Instruments
- Issued exclusively by registered NPOs on SSE.
- **Characteristics:** Carries **0% interest/coupon** and **zero principal repayment** (acts as a transparent, auditable philanthropic donation).
- **Issue Parameters:**
  - Minimum Issue Size: **₹50 Lakh** (reduced from ₹1 Crore).
  - Minimum Application / Subscription Size: **₹10,000** (reduced from ₹2 Lakh to encourage retail participation).
- **Social Audit Requirement:** Mandatory annual audit by qualified Social Auditors certified under **NISM Series-XXIII (Social Audit Certification)**.

# MODULE 6: CAPITAL MARKETS, EXTERNAL TRADE & FISCAL DYNAMICS

---

## 6.1 Special Rupee Vostro Accounts (SRVA) & INR Internationalisation
*Regulatory Reference: RBI/2022-2023/90 A.P. (DIR Series) Circular No. 10 | Framework under FEMA 1999*

### A. Mechanism of SRVA
To promote global trade in Indian Rupees (INR) and reduce reliance on third-party reserve currencies (USD, EUR), the RBI established the SRVA mechanism:
1. An overseas correspondent bank opens an **INR-denominated Special Rupee Vostro Account** with an Authorized Dealer Category-I (AD Category-I) bank in India.
2. **Import Settlements:** Indian importers pay in INR into the overseas bank's SRVA against supply invoices.
3. **Export Settlements:** Indian exporters receive payments in INR debited directly from the overseas bank's SRVA.
4. **Surplus Funds Utilization:** Any surplus INR balance held in the SRVA can be invested in **Government Securities (G-Secs), Treasury Bills (T-Bills)**, or used for local payments in India.

### B. Jan 2025/2026 Liberalization
- **Elimination of Prior RBI Approval:** AD Category-I banks can now open SRVAs for overseas correspondent banks **autonomously under delegated powers** without referring each case to RBI.
- **SNRA Cross-Settlement:** Balances in Special Non-resident Rupee Accounts (SNRA) and SRVAs can be cross-utilized to settle legitimate third-country trade and foreign direct investment in non-debt instruments.

---

## 6.2 Merchanting Trade Transactions (MTT) Revised Rules
*Regulatory Reference: RBI Guidelines on Merchanting Trade under Foreign Exchange Management Act*

### A. Definition of MTT
A transaction where goods are purchased from a supplier resident in Country A and shipped directly to a buyer in Country B without the goods ever physically entering the domestic tariff territory of India, with an Indian intermediary merchant managing the transaction.

### B. Revised Timelines:
- **Foreign Currency Outlay Period:** The maximum period permitted between making the foreign currency payment for imports and receiving the foreign currency proceeds from exports is extended from **4 months to 6 months**.
- **Overall Completion Period:** The entire merchanting trade cycle must be completed within an overall period of **9 months**.
- **Letter of Credit (LC) Rule:** Third-party payments and advance remittances are strictly regulated to prevent round-tripping.

---

## 6.3 Specialized Investment Funds (SIF) — SEBI New Asset Class
*Regulatory Reference: SEBI Board Notification | Formalized: December 16, 2024*

### A. Strategic Positioning
SIF is a new regulated investment vehicle designed to bridge the structural gap between **Mutual Funds** (retail focus, ₹500 SIP, conservative risk) and **Portfolio Management Services (PMS)** (HNIs, statutory minimum ₹50 Lakh).

### B. Key Parameters
- **Minimum Investment Ticket Size:** **₹10 Lakh** per investor.
- **Risk Profile:** High-risk sophisticated investors.
- **Permitted Strategies:** Long-short equity strategies, inverse ETFs, unlisted debt derivatives, structured derivative products with robust portfolio-level risk caps.
- **Sponsor Eligibility:** AMC must have a minimum 3-year track record and average AUM of ₹10,000 Crore, or employ experienced fund managers with proven derivative track records.

- **Zero Commission Friction:** Fully digital onboarding and zero transaction charges via NPCI AutoPay and UPI 2.0 mandate integrations.
